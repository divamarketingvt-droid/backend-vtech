                <div id="faq1" class="accordion-collapse collapse show" data-bs-parent="#faqAccordion">
                  <div class="accordion-body">
                    Employment background screening is the process of verifying a candidate's identity, employment history, education, criminal records and work eligibility before hiring. Employers use background screening to reduce hiring risks, maintain workplace safety, and make more informed hiring decisions. Depending on the role and industry, screening may include criminal background checks, employment verification, education verification, reference checks, drug testing, identity verification and credit history checks. A compliant background screening process helps organizations hire with greater confidence while supporting legal and regulatory requirements.
                  </div>
                </div>
              </div>

              <div class="accordion-item">
                <h2 class="accordion-header">
                  <button class="accordion-button collapsed" type="button" data-bs-toggle="collapse" data-bs-target="#faq2">
                    What Does an FCRA-Compliant Background Screening Include?
                  </button>
                </h2>
                <div id="faq2" class="accordion-collapse collapse" data-bs-parent="#faqAccordion">
                  <div class="accordion-body">
                    An FCRA-compliant background screening follows the requirements and other applicable employment laws. Typically, an FCRA-compliant screening includes:
                    <ul>
                      <li>Candidate consent and authorization</li>
                      <li>Clear disclosure before screening</li>
                      <li>Criminal background checks</li>
                      <li>Employment and education verification</li>
                      <li>Identity verification</li>
                      <li>Adverse action procedures when required</li>
                      <li>Secure handling of candidate information</li>
                    </ul>
                    Employers must ensure screening is conducted fairly, transparently, and in compliance with federal and state employment regulations.
                  </div>
                </div>
              </div>

                <div id="faq3" class="accordion-collapse collapse" data-bs-parent="#faqAccordion">

                      <li><strong>3-5 Business Days</strong> → Complex or international verifications</li>

                </h2>
                <div id="faq5" class="accordion-collapse collapse" data-bs-parent="#faqAccordion">
                  <div class="accordion-body">
                    E-Verify requirements vary by employer type and state law. While E-Verify is not mandatory for all private employers nationwide, it may be required for:
                    <ul>

              <div class="accordion-item">
                <h2 class="accordion-header">
                  <button class="accordion-button collapsed" type="button" data-bs-toggle="collapse" data-bs-target="#faq6">
                    What Is Form I-9?
                  </button>
                </h2>
                <div id="faq6" class="accordion-collapse collapse" data-bs-parent="#faqAccordion">
                  <div class="accordion-body">
                    Form I-9 (Employment Eligibility Verification) is a federal document used to confirm that an employee is authorized to work in the United States. Employers must complete Form I-9 for every employee hired in the US. The process includes:
                    <ul>
                      <li><strong>Section 1</strong> - Employee identity and work authorization information</li>
                      <li><strong>Section 2</strong> - Employer review of identity and employment eligibility documents</li>

        anchor.addEventListener("click", function (e) {
          e.preventDefault();
          const target = document.querySelector(this.getAttribute("href"));
          if (target) {
            const offsetTop = target.offsetTop - 100;
            window.scrollTo({
              top: offsetTop,
              behavior: "smooth",
            });
          }
        });
      });
    </script>
  </body>
</html>
